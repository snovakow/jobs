GROUP B — STRONG FIT, WOULD PROBABLY BE FILTERED PAST.
------------------------------------------------------

Every title here describes work already on the resume under a name that does
not read as senior software engineering. These are where the least competition
sits, and several of them pay at or above the equivalent engineer title.

- Creative Technologist / Senior Creative Technologist / Lead Creative
  Technologist — FILTER PAST. At agencies and experiential studios this is the
  senior WebGL role, and the portfolio, the broadcaster credits and the Poly
  Awards are exactly the hiring evidence these postings ask for.
- Media Systems Engineer / Media Technology Engineer / Broadcast Systems
  Developer — FILTER PAST. Reads as operations; at Evertz, Ross Video, CBC and
  Bell a large share of these are software roles on playout, transport and
  player stacks.
- Technical Director — FILTER PAST. In studio and agency usage this is an
  engineering leadership title, not a film credit, and the eleven years of
  owning a platform frontend is the profile.
- Solutions Engineer / Solutions Architect / Sales Engineer at video and 3D
  vendors — FILTER PAST. Customer-facing, but the technical bar at Bitmovin,
  Mux, Esri and Unity-class vendors is real integration work, and the pay is
  frequently above the equivalent product-engineering band.
- Developer Advocate / Developer Relations Engineer at WebGL, XR and video
  vendors — FILTER PAST. The awards, the conference-grade demos and the Quest
  browser splash-page placement are the portfolio this hires on.
- Research Engineer / Research Software Engineer in visualization or graphics —
  FILTER PAST. Four peer-reviewed publications and six years on a funded
  university project are a credential most candidates for these roles do not
  have.
- Forward Deployed Engineer / Implementation Engineer at media and geospatial
  platforms — FILTER PAST. Integration-heavy product work under a
  services-sounding name.
- Prototyper / Senior Prototyper / Experience Prototyper
- Full Stack Developer, Interactive Media

GROUP C — PLAUSIBLE, BUT READ THE POSTING BEFORE SPENDING THE HOUR.
-------------------------------------------------------------------

- Senior Frontend Engineer / Staff Frontend Engineer with no qualifier — the
  volume of the market. Worth applying to only where the product has a
  rendering, media, visualization or real-time collaboration surface. React and
  testing screens bite hardest here.
- Senior Full Stack Engineer — same test, and the backend half is PHP/MySQL and
  Node build tooling on this resume, not a services platform.
- Software Engineer, Tools / Tools Programmer / Pipeline Engineer — studio
  tooling, usually Python-gated and often C++-gated.
- Technical Artist / Technical Art Director — DCC-gated (Houdini, Maya,
  Blender) and the resume carries no DCC work.
- Rendering Engineer / Graphics Software Engineer / Engine Programmer — the
  work matches and the language does not; these are C++ reqs with rare
  exceptions.
- Digital Twin Developer / Simulation Engineer — transferable, but usually
  Unreal, C++ or a domain-engineering background.
- Geospatial Developer / GIS Application Developer — transferable, with a
  mapping-domain expectation the resume does not carry.
- Mobile Engineer, iOS (cross-platform shops) — screens on Swift concurrency,
  SwiftUI and current Apple frameworks rather than on Objective-C and OpenGL
  ES2.

GROUP D — TITLES TO IGNORE DESPITE KEYWORD OVERLAP.
---------------------------------------------------

- 3D Artist, Motion Designer, VFX Artist, Unity Developer (games) — content
  production, not engineering.
- Video Editor, Post-Production Engineer, Broadcast Operator — operations roles
  that match on "video" and nothing else.
- AR/VR Producer, XR Project Manager — delivery management.
- Machine Learning Engineer, 3D Vision — reconstruction postings that want
  CUDA, PyTorch and usually a graduate degree.

ITEM 4 — MARKET POSITION, BLUNTLY
===============================================================================

WHERE ELEVEN YEARS AT ONE SMALL COMPANY HELPS
---------------------------------------------

- One codebase across three generations. The internal WebGL rendering library,
  its migration to Three.js, and the 70,000-line TypeScript port are the same
  player rewritten three times. Very few candidates can show a decade of
  continuous ownership through two full technology shifts, and it answers the
  maintenance question better than any product count does — the codebase
  changed under the engineer rather than the engineer moving between codebases.
- Third-party adoption, not internal usage. ARTE, ZDF, BBC and AT&T authored
  their own productions on the platform. External organizations choosing to
  build on a frontend is materially stronger evidence than shipping features an
  internal stakeholder requested, and it is rare on a small-company resume.
- External validation with numbers attached. Three 2021 WebXR Poly Awards, the
  Meta Quest browser splash page, roughly 1,000 visits a day, sixteen episodes
  kept stable on Quest 2 memory. Agencies, studios and XR employers hire on
  exactly this evidence.
- Credible breadth. Web, native iOS and tvOS, headset, a PHP/MySQL analytics
  backend, real-time multiplayer. At a large company that spread would read as
  padding; in a small platform company it is believable, and it is why the
  frontend-with-depth segment lands at all.
- Real systems work under the frontend title. A twenty-participant shared
  session with presence, video avatars, screen sharing, pinned comment threads
  and a media-server migration off peer-to-peer is distributed-systems work,
  not UI work. Say it in those terms.
- Two employers with six and eleven years. Nobody will ask about job hopping,
  and the university years carry publications and a funded mandate that most
  industry resumes cannot match.

WHERE IT HURTS
--------------

- No external calibration of level. Eleven years in one small shop means no
  promotion history against a rubric, no experience of another engineering
  culture, and no evidence of code review, on-call, or working inside a large
  codebase owned by other people. The concern is never capability; it is
  whether the work holds up under someone else's constraints.
- Frontend Lead at a small company and Staff Engineer at a 200-person company
  are different signals. Expect to be levelled Senior rather than Staff
  anywhere above roughly a hundred engineers, and expect an interviewer to ask
  when a more senior engineer last reviewed a design — the resume's own
  phrasing is "challenged platform design decisions with management", which
  reads as having been the most senior technical voice in the room.
- No testing or CI anywhere. In a small shop that is normal; on a senior
  frontend req it is the single most frequently listed requirement the resume
  cannot evidence, and it reads as a habit gap rather than a knowledge gap. It
  also undercuts the strongest modernization claim — see the port question
  below.
- Compensation anchoring. A decade at one small company usually means a
  below-market base, and Canadian recruiters ask current compensation early.
  Decide a number before the first screen and quote market rather than history.
- Niche-platform risk. Interactive 360° cinema is a small industry with few

WHAT A HIRING MANAGER WILL PROBE
--------------------------------

- "Why now, after eleven years?" This gets asked first, every time. It needs a
  forward-looking answer about the kind of problem wanted next, not an account
  of what went wrong.
- Level calibration. Expect "what did you design that someone else had to
  maintain?" and "when were you overruled by another engineer, and what
  happened?" Both are aimed at the most-senior-in-the-room pattern.
- "How did you know the port didn't break anything?" The 70,000-line TypeScript
  port is the strongest modernization evidence on the resume and the place the
  missing test suite is most visible. Answer it directly — module-by-module
  review, manual QA against the broadcaster productions, what would be done
  differently — rather than being surprised by it.
- Scepticism about the agents. Expect an implicit "did you do this, or did
  Claude?" The per-file agent-switching detail is the answer, plus one concrete
  module where the agent's output was rejected and why. Agent-first development
  is currently a differentiator; it turns into a liability the moment it sounds
  like delegation.
- "Where have you used React in production?" There is no good evasion. The
  answer is nowhere yet, followed immediately by the comparable thing — an
  70,000-line component-architected TypeScript player — and by
  react-three-fiber if it has been closed by then.
- Scale, in absolute terms. A thousand visits a day and twenty concurrent
  participants are small numbers. Frame them as constraint-driven targets
  (Quest 2 memory ceilings, broadcast production requirements) rather than as
  traffic achievements, because framed as achievements they invite an
  unfavourable comparison.
- Testing and CI practice, asked as a process question rather than a tooling
  question. Have a considered answer about what would be put in place in the
  first month.

DOES THE CURRENT FRAMING HOLD UP
--------------------------------

Mostly, with one overclaim that is worth fixing because it is checkable from
the resume itself.

- "Five distinct products" overclaims, and the resume shows why. Three products
  and two subsystems is the honest shape: the web player (with the WebGL
  library, the Three.js migration, the TypeScript port, 360°/stereoscopic
  video, WebXR and splatting all as layers on it), the native iOS and tvOS
  clients, and the collaborative platform. WebXR support is a mode of the
  player, and the WebRTC multi-user video is a subsystem of the collaborative
  platform — a reader who has the resume in front of them will see that, and
  the correction costs more than the extra two claims earn. "Three products
  across one codebase lineage, plus the analytics backend" is both defensible
  and stronger.
- The lineage framing beats the product count outright. The same player
  rewritten three times across eleven years answers "was this a decade of
  maintenance?" more convincingly than a list does, because it puts the change
  inside the codebase rather than in a count of deliverables. Lead with it.
- Gaussian splatting belongs at the front of the resume, not in the product
  list. It is not a product, and it is the single most current and most
  differentiating line in the document — the only one that places this work in
  the 2025–26 conversation rather than the 2021 one.
- The agent-driven TypeScript port does belong in the list, at product scale,
  but only with a verification story attached. Unqualified, "ported 70,000
  lines with AI agents" invites the scepticism described above; with the
  per-file agent switching and the review process, it becomes the strongest
  modernization evidence available.
- Blockly visual scripting stays off the leading list. It is unshipped and the
  market for visual-scripting authoring is narrow. It earns one line at engine
  and tooling employers — SideFX above all, where node-based authoring is the
  product — and nowhere else.
- Leading with ARTE, ZDF, BBC and AT&T works for broadcast, agency and XR
  audiences and works against the resume in product-engineering interviews,
  where a client list reads as agency delivery work. For segment 3 targets,
  lead instead with the TypeScript port and the twenty-participant shared
  session.
- The three 2021 Poly Awards are five years old now. Still worth leading with
  at studios and XR employers; at product companies they date the work unless
  paired with splatting and the port, so that the timeline reads 2021 through
  2026 rather than stopping in 2021.
- The Cumming School anatomy tools are underused in the current framing. They
  are supporting evidence for XR and agency applications, and they are the lead
  credential for cluster 4, which is the most under-exploited cluster on this
  resume.
- The Fraunhofer HHI OMAF integration is correctly placed as supporting
  evidence, with one exception: for video infrastructure postings it should be
  promoted, because standards-body integration work is the credential that
  distinguishes a player engineer from a frontend engineer who has touched
  video.
