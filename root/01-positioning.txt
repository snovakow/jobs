(Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday) and aggregators match literal strings, so
framework and standard names are the precise instrument there, including names
LinkedIn's index does not hold. LinkedIn job search is semantic; its four-term
sets follow the four keyword tests and keyword-edits.txt, which governs the
LinkedIn sets only. Each board-search list below is carried verbatim in the
matching 02-segment file.

LINKEDIN — FOUR SETS, FOUR KEYWORDS EACH, NARROWEST FIRST
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Set n covers segment n's territory. Both files of a set carry the same four
keywords in the same order.

SET 1 — GRAPHICS / XR
  WebXR, Three.js, OpenGL, augmented reality
  - WebXR leads: the narrowest term, and the resume's most direct evidence
    (Magic of Flight; WebXR support in the player). Recorded replacement, kept.
  - Three.js: the library the player migrated to. Recorded replacement, kept.
  - OpenGL: replaces "3D rendering" (see REJECTED THIS RUN). Reaches native
    graphics roles, a pool the web terms do not reach; evidenced by the anatomy
    app and the vaccine-design visualizations. Expect C++-gated postings among
    them.
  - augmented reality: broadest, last. Recorded replacement, kept.

SET 2 — VIDEO INFRASTRUCTURE
  video player, transcoding, WebRTC, video streaming
  - video player leads: player-side work is the segment's priority and the
    resume's core product. Recorded replacement, kept.
  - transcoding: fills the slot FFmpeg held until LinkedIn returned no matching
    jobs for it; a kind-of-work term rather than a tool name, evidenced by the
    encoding work for ARTE.
  - WebRTC: real-time media, evidenced by the multi-user video work.
  - video streaming: broadest, last.

SET 3 — SENIOR FRONTEND WITH DEPTH
  real-time collaboration, web performance, data visualization, TypeScript
  - All four are recorded replacements, kept. real-time collaboration leads as
    the narrow term the shared-session work evidences. TypeScript sits last as
    the broadest and is the term most likely to cross about 100 close matches
    on the Toronto pass; the run's KEYWORD EDIT line reports that when it
    happens.

SET 4 — APPLE PLATFORMS
  video playback, spatial computing, smart TV, mobile video
  - Built from kind-of-work terms: the record rules out Apple framework and
    platform names as a family. video playback leads (the native clients and
    the player). spatial computing covers 3D and XR work of the visionOS kind;
    smart TV covers tvOS and connected-TV apps; mobile video is broadest, last.
    video playback and mobile video are recorded replacements, kept; SwiftUI
    and native iOS are removed for aim.

REJECTED THIS RUN
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- "3D rendering" (set 1, recorded 2026-09-17): semantic matching pulls 3D
  artist, visualizer and design postings — architectural, interior and product
  rendering — far more than rendering engineering. Replaced with OpenGL.
- "HLS" (set 2): a niche video-standard name after WebCodecs, OMAF and FFmpeg
  failed, and an acronym shared with unrelated fields (FPGA high-level
  synthesis; health and life sciences consulting). Replaced with video
  streaming.
- "live streaming" (set 2): a large non-technical market — streamers,
  live-event and social production. Replaced with video streaming.
- "SwiftUI" (set 4): footprint without aim; pulls roles built around native app
  development. Replaced with spatial computing.
- "native iOS" (set 4): aims at native iOS roles. Replaced with smart TV.
- "iOS" (set 4): aims at native iOS roles. Replaced with mobile video.
- "connected TV" (set 4): dominated by CTV advertising sales, and in Canada it
  collides with the CTV network. Replaced with smart TV.
- "3D visualization" (set 4): pulls architectural and product 3D visualizer
  roles. Replaced with spatial computing.
- "medical imaging" (set 4): a large non-software market (imaging
  technologists, sonographers). Replaced with spatial computing.

The same rejections, in the step 1 rejection form for keyword-edits.txt, are in
the chat reply for this run.

THE DECADE AT ONE SMALL COMPANY — WHERE IT HELPS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Long-horizon architectural judgement, which most senior candidates cannot
  show. One engineer carried a 70,000-line player from a hand-written WebGL
  renderer through a Three.js migration, WebXR, shared multi-user sessions and
  a TypeScript port, and lived with each decision afterwards. Candidates who
  move every two or three years rarely see their own decisions come due.
- External names that carry the screen. Meta (Magic of Flight, the Quest
  browser splash page, three 2021 Poly Awards) and ARTE, ZDF and BBC shipping
  their own work on the platform do for a small company what a large employer's
  brand does for other candidates.
- The exact profile small studios, agencies and startups hire: one senior who
  owns a whole surface — renderer, player, native clients, analytics backend,
  real-time networking — and mentors.

THE DECADE AT ONE SMALL COMPANY — WHERE IT HURTS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- No scale or process signals. The resume shows no team size, test suite, CI,
  code review, design documents or usage figures for the platform itself; the
  only usage numbers are Magic of Flight's roughly 1,000 visits a day and the
  anatomy app's roughly 2,000 downloads a year. Larger employers screen on
  those signals and read their absence as a small team with informal process.
- Levelling. "Senior Developer, Frontend Lead" at a small company reads as
  senior individual contributor. Staff and principal postings at mid-size and
  large companies ask for influence across teams; the resume shows influence
  across one small company's technical leads. Senior is the realistic level at
  large companies. Staff is realistic where the posting is visualization-core
  and depth outweighs organizational scope, mostly at small and mid-size
  companies.
- Tenure. Two decades across two employers — the University of Calgary, then
  Liquid Cinema — raise two questions in every interview: why leave now, and
  how the candidate works inside a larger engineering organization. Both need a
  prepared one-sentence answer.
- Category. The platform's labels are ones the market cooled on. 360° video
  faded as a consumer format, "metaverse" became a liability word after
  2022–23, and XR hiring contracted, with Meta cutting Reality Labs staff
  repeatedly. The skills underneath — real-time 3D in the browser, immersive
  video, shared real-time state — did not cool; the labels did. Leading with
  the labels reads as a bet the market stopped making.

WHERE THE STACK IS DATED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Rendering. WebGL-era: no WebGPU (now shipping in Chrome, Edge and Safari), no
  Three.js WebGPU renderer or TSL, no react-three-fiber. Three.js postings
  increasingly pair it with R3F, WebGPU or both. Closeable in a few weekends
  with one public project; a WebGPU Gaussian-splat viewer built in R3F closes
  all three and doubles as a portfolio piece for the 3D-capture cluster.
- React. The only evidence is a 2026 personal project built to learn it, and
  segment 3 postings screen on production React. Weekends close the syntax, not
  the screen. Name the gap rather than hide it; the TypeScript port leadership
  is the counterweight.
- Testing and CI. Absent, and asked for in almost every senior frontend
  posting. If the player or the port has tests or a pipeline, the resume should
  say so — a wording fix. If not, one public project with Vitest, Playwright
  and a CI workflow closes it on paper in a weekend.
- C++. Absent, and table stakes for native rendering, engine, driver, codec and
  media-pipeline roles. Not closeable in weekends to the level those roles
  screen at; those roles stay stretch whatever the domain fit.
- Streaming vocabulary. The resume shows FFmpeg encoding and Vimeo hosting, but
  none of the terms player-engineering postings are written in: HLS, DASH,
  CMAF, adaptive bitrate, DRM (Widevine, FairPlay), low-latency HLS, hls.js,
  Shaka, ExoPlayer, QoE metrics. If the 360° player streamed HLS or DASH or
  switched bitrates, the resume should say so — a wording gap, not a skills
  gap. If it did not, segment 2 player roles screen it out on vocabulary alone.
- Real-time media. PeerJS and Ant Media Server are integrations. Transport
  roles probe SFU internals, simulcast, bandwidth estimation and congestion
  control. Present the multi-user work as product-level real-time video and
  shared state, not as media-transport engineering.
- Native Apple. Objective-C and Swift from the anatomy-app era; no SwiftUI,
  Metal, RealityKit, ARKit or visionOS. Usable only as evidence that
  visualization shipped on the App Store.
- Backend. PHP and MySQL, no cloud: adequate where the backend serves a
  visualization product, disqualifying for cloud-heavy full-stack roles.
- Current and differentiating: TypeScript at 70,000-line scale, Vite, and
  agent-first development with a result of that size behind it. Many candidates
  now claim AI-assisted development; few can point to a port that large.

WHAT A HIRING MANAGER WILL PROBE

DOES THE FRAMING HOLD UP
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- "Five distinct products" does not survive a careful read. The resume
  describes one platform, and a reader who checks the list against the bullets
  finds padding, which costs more credibility than "one platform" would. Three
  of the five fail:
  - WebXR support is a capability of the player. Magic of Flight is the
    product: named, shipped with Meta, awarded, measured. Replace the one with
    the other.
  - WebRTC multi-user video is part of the collaborative platform; listing both
    counts one product twice.
  - The iOS and tvOS clients were a separate codebase, but the resume also says
    the web player is now the single frontend across desktop, mobile and
    headset, so the clients read as superseded — and leading with them invites
    the native-iOS probe the brief sets out to avoid.

  rebuilt at rewrite scale several times: a custom WebGL renderer migrated to
  Three.js; a single-viewer player turned into shared multi-user sessions; flat
  screens extended to headsets through WebXR; 70,000 lines of JavaScript ported
  to TypeScript; hand-built interactions moving to code-free authoring in
  Blockly and next to AI-guided experiences. Maintenance does not produce
  changes of that size.
- Where a list of shipped things is wanted, four hold up: the web player and

  collaborative platform built with Conestoga College; the broadcaster
  productions (ARTE, ZDF, BBC) and AT&T's authored training content. The native

- Gaussian splatting belongs in the lead as a capability, not in a product
  list. It is the resume's most in-demand item for segment 1 and the 3D-capture
  cluster, and it needs one clause stating what was implemented to survive the
  built-versus-integrated probe.
- The agent-driven TypeScript port belongs in the re-architecture sequence and

- Blockly visual scripting is work in progress, not a shipped product. List it

- ARTE, ZDF, BBC and AT&T pass screens, and "shipped their own work on the
