THE FOUR LINKEDIN SETS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LinkedIn matches semantically, so near-synonyms return the same postings and a
long list buys nothing. Four terms per set, most different from one another,
narrowest first. These are what went into the eight 02-linkedin files.

  Set 1, graphics and XR    | WebXR, Three.js, real-time rendering, WebGL
  Set 2, video infra        | transcoding, WebCodecs, WebRTC, video streaming
  Set 3, frontend depth     | WebAssembly, real-time collaboration,
                            |   data visualization, web performance
  Set 4, Apple platforms    | visionOS, tvOS, AVFoundation, SwiftUI

Two notes on those choices, both worth acting on later rather than now:

  WebCodecs may return zero close matches in Canada for months at a time. It
  is there because it is the one term that finds browser-side media
  engineering specifically, and a term that returns nothing costs almost
  nothing. If it is still empty after three passes, swap it for FFmpeg and log
  the change in keyword-edits.txt.

  "developer tools" was the obvious fourth term for set 3 and is not in it.
  It contains the word developer, which is the most common token in
  engineering postings, and LinkedIn's semantic matcher would very likely
  inflate it into the hundreds of close matches the close-match divider is
  supposed to filter. "web performance" occupies that slot instead: same
  breadth, no title token, and it discriminates deep frontend work from
  forms-and-dashboards work.

  Metal is deliberately absent from set 4. LinkedIn's semantic matcher reads
  it as the material, and a search for it returns fabrication and
  manufacturing jobs. AVFoundation covers the Apple media territory where you
  are actually strong.

HOW TO READ THIS LIST — THE URL RULE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
No searching was done, so every URL below would be recall. You asked for a
conservative list because a guessed URL sends a later Research run to a dead
address and spends budget there.

The rule I applied: a URL appears only where I am confident of the exact
address, which in practice means large companies whose careers page has lived
at the same top-level path for many years. Everything else says "careers URL
unknown", including companies I am confident exist and that you should
absolutely target. An unknown URL is not a weak lead — Ross Video and Evertz
have no URL below and are two of the best fits on the whole list.

The Research sweeps can find a careers page from a company name. They cannot
recover from being sent somewhere wrong.

FLAGS mean: verify before spending time. Where I flag an acquisition or a
shutdown, I am recalling something I believe happened but have not confirmed,
and some of it is close enough to my knowledge cutoff that it may have moved
again.

4.1  THE TWO BEST THINGS ON YOUR RESUME ARE NOT IN YOUR LEAD
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You lead with the Meta collaboration, the Poly Awards and the Fraunhofer HHI
integration. Those are validation. Validation is not the same as evidence of
capability, and you have two pieces of evidence that are stronger than all
three.

The first is one line, buried as the second bullet of your current job:
you built a raw WebGL application on a rendering library you wrote yourself,
and then migrated the product off it onto Three.js. That is two hard things.
Writing a renderer puts you in a different population from people who list
Three.js — most of them have only ever consumed it. Retiring your own library
in favour of a standard one, in a live product, is a judgement call that a lot
of engineers with your background never make correctly. Hiring managers in
this cluster care about exactly that. Right now it reads as a housekeeping
bullet.

The second is the Lindsay Project. Six years of medical 3D, an App Store
application, a web application, a Mac authoring tool, a Springer chapter and
national broadcast coverage. In the medical and scientific visualization
cluster this is a credential, not just experience, and that cluster is small
enough that eleven-year-old work still counts. You are currently treating it
as history.

4.2  WHERE TEN YEARS AT ONE SMALL COMPANY HELPS

- Five product launches is more shipped surface area than most senior

- End-to-end ownership is credible from you in a way it is not from someone

4.3  WHERE IT HURTS, INCLUDING ONE THING YOU DID NOT ASK ABOUT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The thing you did not ask about first: it is not ten years at one company. It
is twenty-one years at two organizations, one of them a university. You have
never worked at a mid-size or large company, you have never been through
another engineering culture, and you have not competed for a job in about a
decade. Every item below follows from that, and it is the real shape of the
risk.

- No external calibration. Nobody knows whether your senior is their senior.
  This is the single biggest source of interview friction you will hit, and
  it is why the levelling question in item 4.5 matters.

- Practices are invisible. There is no testing, no CI, no code review, no
  on-call, no design docs, no staged rollout anywhere on the resume. A
  reviewer does not conclude "probably had those"; they conclude "absent
  until proven otherwise". At a small company some of them genuinely were
  absent, and you need an honest answer ready that does not sound
  defensive.

- No numbers. Not one. No users, no concurrent sessions, no latency figure,
  no frame budget, no encoding throughput, no revenue. In a ten-year tenure
  that omission is loud. It is also the cheapest thing on this list to fix.

- Levelling. Twenty-one years in and the title is still Senior Developer. At
  a company with a real ladder, twenty-one years reads as staff or principal,

- Compensation calibration. Ten years at one small company usually means you
  are below the Toronto market band and do not know by how much. Find out
  before you name a number, because your first offer will anchor the rest.

- Codebase monoculture. Everything you have built follows conventions you
  chose. Expect "when did you last work inside a large codebase somebody else
  designed" and have a real answer.

- Interview mechanics. You have not done a competitive loop in roughly
  eleven years. Algorithm screens in JavaScript or TypeScript, system design
  rounds, and take-homes are all normal now. Budget four to six weeks. Have

4.7  DOES YOUR FRAMING HOLD UP
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Partly. Taking the two halves separately.

"Five distinct products, so it reads as range, not maintenance."
The substance is true. The presentation does not support it. Your Liquid
Cinema section is an undated list of thirteen bullets in which the WebGL
player, the iOS clients, the WebXR support, the WebRTC work and the metaverse
platform all sit at the same visual level as a Vimeo integration. A skeptical
reader sees one product with features added over a decade, which is the exact
reading you are trying to prevent.

The fix is mechanical and it is the highest-value change on this list. Give
each of the five a year range and treat it as its own block with its own
outcome. "2015 to 2018, WebGL 360 player" followed by "2018 to 2020, iOS and
tvOS clients" is a career with phases. The same content undated is a job
description. You are asking the reader to take range on faith when you could
just show it.

Second, be ready for the other reading of five products in ten years at a
small company: that the company kept changing direction and nothing found its
market. Attaching an outcome to each — shipped to whom, used by how many,
what it enabled — closes that off.

"Lead with Meta, the Poly Awards and Fraunhofer HHI."
Three problems.

First, all three are from 2020 and 2021. Your headline evidence is five years
old, and the most recent thing on the resume is the item that has aged worst.
The last five years of your decade are represented by the metaverse platform
and nothing else. A reader reasonably asks what you have been doing since
2021. Fix that with one piece of public 2026 work: a WebGPU or TSL port of
something you already have, an accepted pull request to Three.js, or an
application in a store. One artifact resets the recency read, and nothing else
on this list is as cheap.

Second, each of the three only lands with one audience. Fraunhofer and OMAF
are impressive to broadcast and standards people, which means Evertz, Ross,
Dejero, Bell and the MediaKind class of employer, and are noise everywhere
else. The Poly Awards land inside the WebXR community and read as filler to
anyone outside it, because an unfamiliar award with a superlative name is
indistinguishable from a made-up one. The Meta collaboration is the only one
with brand weight to a general audience, and even there the logo does less
work than a sentence about what was technically hard.

Third, and most important, for segment 3 the whole framing is wrong. To a
hiring manager who needs someone to own a large React and TypeScript codebase,
that lead says "specialist in something I do not have". For those roles lead
with the production browser application shipped and maintained for a decade,
the rendering engine migration performed under a live product, performance
ownership, and the platform breadth. Save the awards for the last paragraph.

So: keep the framing for segments 1, 2 and 4, rebuild the lead for segment 3,
date the five products, and get one recent artifact published.
