INDEED SET RULES, AS APPLIED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each keyword is a phrase real postings use and names a technology, a standard
or a kind of work; none is a generic title and none is an ordinary English word
with a large non-technical job market. Rejected on that rule: Metal (the
canonical trap), canvas (Canvas LMS, art), rendering (print, architecture),
streaming (music, gaming), simulation (training in every field), maps (retail,
logistics), video (every media job) and immersive (hospitality, marketing).
Three.js and real-time collaboration were kept: Indeed tokenizes the period and
the hyphen away and the phrase still matches the way postings write it.
Gaussian splatting was left out of the Indeed set only because a 14-day Indeed
window in Canada almost never carries it; it stays in the sweep keywords and in
LinkedIn description 1.

===============================================================================
4. MARKET POSITION — THE BLUNT READ
===============================================================================

Blunt, with the resume read as a history. Where a point is load-bearing for the
job search it says what to change.


WHERE A DECADE-PLUS AT ONE SMALL COMPANY HELPS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - End-to-end ownership that almost nobody at a large company can claim. You
    wrote the WebGL rendering library, made the call to migrate it to Three.js,
    and carried the same player through 360° and stereoscopic video, WebXR,
    multi-user sessions and Gaussian splatting, with real audiences on the
    other end: ARTE, ZDF, BBC, AT&T, and the Quest browser splash page at about
    1,000 visits a day. Small and mid-size visualization companies
    (Cognitive3D, Mappedin, BioRender, Waabi's tooling teams, the experiential
    studios) hire for exactly that: one person who can hold a whole
    graphics-and-media codebase and ship.

  - The currency curve runs the right way. Most twenty-year resumes trail off;
    yours gets more modern toward the end: Gaussian splatting in production, a
    70,000-line agent-driven TypeScript port, Blockly visual scripting now,
    AI-guided experiences next. That chronology is the strongest single answer
    to the maintenance reading, provided the years are visible (see the framing
    section).

  - Performance stories with numbers. Sixteen episodes kept stable on Quest 2
    by strict memory loading and unloading, sessions tested at 20 concurrent
    participants, the PeerJS-to-Ant-Media migration. Interviewers remember
    specific, measured engineering; keep the numbers on the resume and in the
    first conversation.

  - Stability reads well to small and mid-size employers burned by churn, and
    Frontend Lead with intern mentorship and cross-team guidance is credible at
    senior level everywhere.


WHERE IT HURTS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Scale and process. Nothing on the resume shows work inside a larger
    engineering organization: code review at scale, CI/CD, test suites, release
    trains, on-call, design documents, product-management cycles,
    observability, security review. A hiring manager at a 200-plus-person
    company will assume you have not done it, and the resume gives them nothing
    to argue with. This, not skills, is the most likely reason a strong Tier 1
    fit still comes back as a no: can he operate inside our process.

  - Levelling and compensation. Senior Developer, Frontend Lead at a company
    whose size the resume never states maps to Senior at most large companies,
    not Staff, even if you were the most senior engineer in the building. State
    the scale (team size, user base, the 70,000 lines already on the page) so a
    reader levels you on evidence rather than on the title. Expect
    small-company pay after ten years to sit below the Toronto band for the
    level you are interviewing at, and know the band before you answer the
    expectations question.

  - One context. Ten years of one platform means one architecture, one set of
    trade-offs, one deployment model, and interviewers probe that with what
    would you do differently and what have you seen elsewhere. Your honest
    answers are inside the tenure: peer-to-peer WebRTC to an SFU, a custom
    WebGL library to Three.js, JavaScript to TypeScript, a single-viewer player
    to a shared space. Prepare them as contrasts, not as a timeline.

  - Age and tenure bias is real and unspoken: a 2006 degree and twenty-plus
    years. The mitigation is the same as above: lead with 2024-2026 work and
    let the dates speak.


WHERE THE STACK IS DATED AGAINST CURRENT EXPECTATIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - No C++. Rendering engineer, engine tools, GPU vendor and codec postings are
    C++-gated almost without exception (Digital Extremes, AMD, Evertz
    compression, Ross XPression, CAE, CM Labs). Not closeable in weekends.
    Treat those as stretch however well the graphics half matches.

  - No WebGPU, no react-three-fiber, no Babylon.js. Three.js postings now
    routinely name WebGPU or R3F, and a posting that lists R3F with React and
    TypeScript will read your React as a hobby. WebGPU is the weekend-closeable
    gap and the best talking point you could add: port the splat renderer or a
    volume renderer to WebGPU and put the frame-time comparison on the resume.
    R3F is a week.

  - React. On the skills list, evidenced only by a 2026 personal project the
    resume says was built to learn it. Segment 3 postings screen on production
    React, so expect where did you use React at work in every screen. Answer
    plainly and pivot: the player is framework-free by design because it is
    embedded in other people's pages; the TypeScript port and the Blockly
    editor are the recent application work; the Tims app shows you can learn a
    framework properly on your own.

  - No testing, no CI, anywhere on the resume. For segment 3 that is a blocker
    at many companies; for segments 1, 2 and 4 it is a probe. Write down
    whatever did exist (a browser-and-headset matrix, the episode-transition
    stress runs, customer QA cycles) and start a small test harness on a side
    project so there is a true sentence to say.

  - No cloud, no containers, no Node backend. The PHP and MySQL backend reads
    as 2012; keep it only as the backend that fed the heat maps. This closes
    cloud-heavy full-stack, which the runbook already excludes, and is
    irrelevant to segments 1 and 4.

  - Video. No HLS, DASH, MSE, EME or DRM, no adaptive bitrate, no WebCodecs, no
    SRT, no codec internals on the page. The 360° and stereoscopic work, the
    FFmpeg encoding, the OMAF integration and the WebRTC migration are real,
    but broadcaster and streaming player postings name the standard stack
    explicitly. Learning hls.js and Shaka internals plus MSE and EME is a few
    weekends; codec engineering is not.

  - Apple. Objective-C and Swift, OpenGL ES 2, iOS and tvOS clients from
    undated years; no Metal, SceneKit, RealityKit, ARKit, visionOS or SwiftUI.
    Segment 4 is correctly aimed only at teams whose substance is media or
    visualization and who also ship to the web. Expect it to be the thinnest
    segment every pass.

  - XR. WebXR is deep; native XR (Unity, Unreal, OpenXR, Meta SDK, visionOS) is
    absent, and most Canadian XR engineer postings are Unity or Unreal. Three
    WebXR Poly Awards are a strong differentiator for the minority of roles
    that are browser-based and nearly invisible to the rest.

  - Gaussian splatting is timely and rare on resumes, so it will be probed
    hard. Be ready with specifics: what you implemented versus integrated (a
    library or your own rasterizer), the sorting strategy, memory and streaming
    on Quest versus desktop, the compression and file formats you handle, how
    captures get into the platform, and frame-time numbers. If the true answer
    is integrated a library and tuned it hard, say that and sell the tuning;
    vagueness here costs more than modesty.

  - Agent-first development is a differentiator for forward-leaning teams and a
    red flag for some: Digital Extremes publicly forbids AI in development and
    in hiring, and it will not be alone. The port story is strong because it
    shows judgement: every module reviewed and refactored, agents switched per
    file when one fell short. Keep it framed as I review and own every line,
    never as the agent did it.


WHAT A HIRING MANAGER WILL PROBE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Team size and who else was on the frontend; what you owned and what others
   did. Have a one-sentence answer with numbers.

2. Why ten-plus years, and why leave now. Answer with the product arc, dated,
   and a forward reason (scale, peers, a domain), never a grievance.

3. Production React, testing and CI, as above.

4. Depth on whatever the posting names. Splatting as above; WebXR session
   management, reference spaces, hand input, foveation and Quest memory
   ceilings; WebRTC SFU versus mesh, simulcast, TURN, bandwidth adaptation;
   video projection formats, stereo packing, bitrate ladders, MSE; Three.js
   internals, render loop, draw-call budgets, texture memory, LOD, instancing.

5. How a 70,000-line port was validated without a test suite. Have a real
   answer: types as the safety net, module-by-module review, behaviour
   comparison against the old build, what regressions customers caught and what
   they did not.

6. How you would work inside a ten-person frontend team with a design system,
   pull requests and a release process you do not own.

7. Compensation expectations, per the levelling point above.

8. Agent use, from both directions: the teams that want it will ask how you
   keep quality, the teams that fear it will ask whether you can still write
   code without one.


CLUSTER FIT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------


STRONG FIT

  - XR and spatial computing where the work is browser-based: XR analytics and
    replay viewers, WebXR content, web viewers for captured scenes.
    Cognitive3D-type roles are close to a resume match.

  - Senior frontend with depth (segment 3) where the product is a rendering or
    visualization surface: BioRender's canvas, Mappedin's rendering SDK,
    Waabi's asset tooling, Esri and Ecopia map frontends, digital-twin web
    viewers, node-based editors. Most Tier 1 rows will come from here.

  - Digital agencies and experiential studios. The line from swarm-art
    installations to WebGL productions for broadcasters is what these studios
    hire for. Two caveats: many installation shops now build in Unreal,
    TouchDesigner or Notch rather than the browser, and agency pay and project
    cadence are a step down from product companies.

  - Medical and scientific visualization in the browser. The anatomy atlas, the
    App Store app and Presenter make a credible story, if a decade old;
    browser-based surgical planning, 3D anatomy and scientific-data viewers
    fit. Device-regulated C++ and Qt roles do not.


MODERATE FIT

  - Simulation and digital twin: strong for the web viewer and tooling side
    (Waabi's simulation tooling, Prevu3D, iTwin-style viewers, Omniverse on the
    web), stretch for the simulation core (CAE, CM Labs, Presagis are C++).

  - Video infrastructure and streaming: player-side and immersive video roles
    fit and you will be screened on HLS, DASH and DRM; pipeline and codec roles
    are C++ or Go gated.

  - Mapping and geospatial: WebGL camera, scene and performance skills transfer
    directly to Mapbox GL, MapLibre, deck.gl and Cesium work; no GIS vocabulary
    on the resume, so expect a gap note on every row.

  - 3D capture and reconstruction: fit on the viewer and renderer side (splat
    and point-cloud viewers, capture-to-web pipelines), not on photogrammetry
    or SLAM.


STRETCH

  - Game and engine tooling (C++, Vulkan, DirectX 12, Metal, Unreal and Unity
    source): only where the posting is web-facing.

  - GPU vendors (AMD, NVIDIA): driver and kernel work; only web tooling or
    Omniverse-on-the-web postings are plausible.

  - Apple platforms (segment 4): only media and visualization apps with a web
    sibling; thin by design.

  - Native XR studios (Unity, Unreal): only where they also ship WebXR or need
    a web viewer.


DOES THE FRAMING HOLD UP
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Mostly, with four changes.

1. Five distinct products holds only if each item is a product with its own
   users and ship date, and two of the five are not: WebXR support is a
   capability of the player, and WebRTC multi-user video is a capability of the

   Re-cut it so each item has a customer and an outcome: (1) the WebGL 360° and

2. Gaussian splatting, the TypeScript port and Blockly do not belong in the
   products list; they are currency evidence and belong in a second, dated
   line: since 2024, Gaussian splatting in production, a 70,000-line

3. Date everything inside 2015 to present. Without years the arc is invisible
   and a reader cannot tell range from maintenance; with years (player, iOS and
   tvOS, analytics, WebXR 2020-21, metaverse from late 2022, splatting and the

   cheapest fix on the list and the one with the most leverage.

4. Leading with ARTE, ZDF, BBC and AT&T is right for media, broadcaster, agency
   and experiential audiences and wrong for engineering-first audiences

   Everywhere else OMAF stays supporting; outside video infrastructure almost

A smaller fifth point: agent-first is on every resume now. What makes yours
credible is the port's specificity, so keep the mechanism (every module
reviewed, agents switched per file) and the size (70,000 lines) in every
version of the pitch.

Net: a strong, distinctive candidate for a narrow market. The constraints are

work is small, so expect thin Tier 1s that are nonetheless real; the fix for
