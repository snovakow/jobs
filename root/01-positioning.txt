1. TITLE TAXONOMY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every job title actually used in postings that maps to this background, grouped
by how well the underlying work fits. Canadian employers often title the same
job "Developer" rather than "Engineer" — engineer is a regulated title in
Canada and many companies avoid it — so treat the two as interchangeable when
searching.

A. STRONGEST FIT — THE TITLE AND THE WORK BOTH MATCH

Graphics and XR:
- WebGL Engineer; WebGL Developer
- Web Graphics Engineer; Senior Software Engineer, Graphics — when the stack is
  the browser or real-time 3D rather than native C++
- Three.js Developer; 3D Web Developer; 3D Web Engineer
- Real-Time 3D Engineer; Real-Time Graphics Engineer
- XR Engineer; WebXR Engineer; VR Engineer; AR/VR Software Engineer; Immersive
  Technology Developer

Video:
- Video Player Engineer; Web Video Player Engineer; Playback Engineer; Media
  Playback Engineer
- Streaming Engineer; Video Streaming Engineer — client or player side
- WebRTC Engineer; Real-Time Communications Engineer; Software Engineer,
  Real-Time Video

Frontend with depth:
- Senior Frontend Engineer; Senior Front-End Developer — when the product is a
  canvas, editor, map, visualization or media surface
- Frontend Lead; Lead Front-End Developer; Frontend Tech Lead
- Senior UI Engineer at graphics, mapping or media companies
- Data Visualization Engineer; Visualization Developer

Apple platforms:
- Senior iOS Engineer; Senior iOS Developer — on video, streaming or media
  products
- tvOS Developer; Apple TV Developer — rare as standalone titles, usually
  folded into an iOS role

B. GOOD FIT, EASY TO FILTER PAST — THE WORK MATCHES, THE TITLE HIDES IT

- Creative Technologist; Creative Developer; Interactive Developer — agency,
  studio and brand-lab titles for real-time 3D, WebGL and installation work.
  Seniority and pay vary widely and some are junior roles, so read the posting.
- Technical Director, Interactive; Interactive Technical Director; Technical
  Lead, Experiential — agency leadership titles for the same work.
- Prototyper; Design Technologist; UX Engineer, Prototyping — XR and 3D
  prototyping inside product and research teams.
- Developer Advocate; Developer Relations Engineer; Solutions Engineer — at 3D,
  mapping, WebRTC and video-SDK companies, where the job is sample apps, demos
  and integrations. The Three.js, Colyseus and Ant Media history maps directly.
- Technical Consultant — at graphics-software vendors; SideFX has used the
  title.
- Media Systems Engineer; Video Systems Developer; Broadcast Software Developer
  — broadcast and media-company titles. Some are software development, some are
  systems integration (IP video routing, hardware); the duties decide.
- Software Developer, Video and Graphics — Evertz's wording; similar hybrid
  titles appear at other broadcast vendors.
- Simulation Software Developer; Simulation Engineer, visualization side
  — simulation and digital-twin companies.
- Research Software Developer; Research Engineer; Scientific Software Developer
  — hospitals, research institutes and universities building imaging,
  visualization and surgical-planning tools. The Lindsay Project history maps
  directly.
- Educational Technology Developer; Learning Experience Developer — edtech and
  medical-education products with 3D.
- Multiplayer Engineer; Real-Time Collaboration Engineer — where the
  shared-state and live-video work now lives; "Metaverse Engineer" has mostly
  disappeared as a title.
- Product Engineer; Design Engineer — at canvas, editor and design-tool
  companies these are often the senior frontend role under another name.

C. PARTIAL FIT — WORTH READING, USUALLY ONE REQUIREMENT SHORT

- Senior Full-Stack Engineer on media-heavy products — backend depth expected;
  Node reads fine, PHP reads old.
- Senior Mobile Developer, iOS-first — check that the role is native-first
  rather than React Native or Flutter first.
- Frontend Platform Engineer; Web Platform Engineer — performance and build
  tooling; strong on performance, weak on the testing and CI these roles
  assume.
- Staff Frontend Engineer — the work fits; the level will be probed (see E).
- AI Product Engineer; Applied AI Engineer, frontend — AI-guided experiences
  and agent-first development; a young category where the agent-driven
  TypeScript port is real evidence.

D. STRETCH — THE TITLE FITS, THE POSTING USUALLY GATES ON WHAT IS MISSING

- Rendering Engineer; Graphics Programmer; Engine Programmer — C++ with Vulkan,
  DirectX or Metal.
- GPU Software Engineer; Graphics Driver Engineer — C++ and driver internals.
- Unity Developer; Unreal Engine Developer — engine experience the resume does
  not show.
- Video Codec Engineer; Video Encoding Engineer; Compression Engineer — C/C++
  and codec internals.
- Media Pipeline Engineer; Video Infrastructure Engineer — backend Go, C++ or
  Rust.
- visionOS Engineer; RealityKit Engineer; ARKit Engineer; Metal Engineer.
- Computer Vision Engineer; 3D Reconstruction Engineer; Research Engineer,
  Neural Rendering — ML depth. Gaussian splatting is relevant on the rendering
  side, not the reconstruction side.
- Senior React Engineer — screens on production React.
- Technical Artist — art-side skills first; tool-building versions (Houdini,
  pipeline) sit closer but still lean on C++ or Python.

E. LEVEL LABELS — HOW TO READ THEM FOR THIS RESUME

- Senior — the natural level at large companies.
- Staff / Principal — the work fits; the leveling signal (one small employer,
  small team) does not. Apply, but expect to be leveled at Senior at large
  companies. At startups and studios, Staff is sometimes simply the senior-most
  engineer.
- Lead / Tech Lead / Frontend Lead — matches the current title; at large
  companies it often implies cross-team scope or people management.
- Engineering Manager, frontend, player or XR — plausible at small companies
  given the lead history; the resume shows little people management, so it is a
  stretch at scale.

2. KEYWORD SETS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two kinds of search use these, and they behave differently.

- Board and ATS searches (company career pages, Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby,
  Workable, Built In, Wellfound) are mostly literal: a niche standard's name
  finds exactly the postings that mention it, so niche terms are an asset
  there. The lists below are for those searches, and each 02-segment file
  carries its segment's list.
- LinkedIn search is semantic and runs on its own index. Niche framework and
  platform names come back empty or get silently swapped for another term, bare
  titles return the whole market, and ordinary English words pull in unrelated
  jobs. The sixteen LinkedIn keywords at the end of this section are chosen
  under those rules.

keyword-edits.txt governs the LinkedIn keywords only. The terms it rules out
— WebCodecs, visionOS, tvOS, AVFoundation, OMAF, Gaussian splatting, Unity,
CRDT, Metal, AVPlayer, FFmpeg, developer tools and senior frontend engineer
— failed or were rejected as LinkedIn keywords. Several stay in the board lists
below, because literal board search handles them well.

LINKEDIN KEYWORD SETS — THE SIXTEEN IN THE 02-LINKEDIN FILES

Four keywords per set, one set per segment, identical in the toronto and remote
files of a set. Each keyword passed four tests: it names a technology, a
standard or a kind of work, never a job title; it contains no title word
(developer, engineer, manager, architect, analyst, designer, lead); it is not
an ordinary English word with a large non-technical job market; and it is
common enough in posting bodies that LinkedIn's index holds it. Every
replacement recorded in keyword-edits.txt was kept, since each still passes
those tests, except FFmpeg, which LinkedIn could not match on either pass.
Within a set the four terms were picked to differ from one another, and they
run narrowest first. The first term in each set names work the resume shows, so
a remote pass that only reaches one keyword spends its opens on postings triage
will keep.

In the table, "recorded" marks a replacement already in keyword-edits.txt.

Why each failed:
- WebGPU — the index test: a young web API with a small posting footprint, the
  WebCodecs failure mode.
- creative technologist — a bare job title.
- broadcast — an ordinary English word with a large non-technical market in
  broadcast journalism and production.
- canvas — an ordinary English word with large unrelated markets: canvassing
  work and the Canvas learning platform.
- node-based editor — "editor" pulls in video, copy and managing editors.
- Blockly — the index test: a library name with a tiny posting footprint.
- Swift — collides with SWIFT payments work in banking and with the adjective.
- UIKit — an Apple framework name; keyword-edits.txt records repeated Apple
  framework and platform failures, and SwiftUI is the one framework name with
  the footprint to stay eligible.
- Apple TV — the same family as tvOS, which returned no matching jobs.

4. MARKET POSITION — THE BLUNT READ
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Short version: you are a rare, strong fit for a small number of roles, and a
middling paper fit for the large pool of senior frontend roles where most
openings actually are. The asset is the combination — browser real-time 3D,
WebXR on Quest, 360°/stereo video and real-time multi-user sessions, shipped in
production by one person with named clients. The liability is that everything
since 2015 sits inside one small company: none of it is corroborated by a
second employer, and it comes without the process markers — tests, CI,
production React, code review at scale — that larger hiring loops screen on
first.

WHERE A DECADE AT ONE SMALL COMPANY HELPS

- Ownership: you led the frontend of a whole platform, not a slice of one.
  Studios, startups and small teams hiring their first senior graphics person
  want exactly that.
- Stewardship: you wrote a WebGL renderer, then migrated it to Three.js; ported
  70,000 lines to TypeScript; kept sixteen episodes stable on Quest 2. Few
  candidates can show a codebase carried through that many generations.
- Outside proof: the Poly Awards, the Quest browser splash page and ~1,000
  visits a day partly substitute for an unknown employer name.

WHERE IT HURTS

- Leveling: "Senior Developer, Frontend Lead" at a small company reads as
  Senior, not Staff. The leadership evidence is one intern, advising other
  leads, and pushing back on management. Expect Senior at large companies and
  to argue for more.
- Scale: nothing shows work inside a large engineering org. The first probe
  will be team size, and the answer anchors everything after it.
- Corroboration: one set of references since 2015, and ARTE, ZDF, BBC and AT&T
  were the platform's customers, not yours.
- "Why now, after eleven years?" Everyone will ask, and some will silently
  wonder whether the company is winding down.
- The metaverse label: it dates the work to the 2021–22 hype, and XR budgets
  have contracted since; Unity alone went through three layoff rounds in a year
  and planned to roughly halve its office count. The work underneath — shared
  real-time state, multi-party video — is current. Rename it.

WHERE THE STACK IS DATED

- No C++ — table stakes for rendering and video-infrastructure postings.
- No WebGPU or react-three-fiber. WebGPU is now shipping in the major browsers,
  and this is the weekend-closeable gap.
- React only in a 2026 personal project.
- No testing or CI anywhere.
- Apple: no SwiftUI, AVFoundation, Metal, RealityKit or visionOS, and the
  iOS/tvOS work is undated. If those clients used AVPlayer, HLS or FairPlay,
  name them — silence reads as "didn't".
- PHP/MySQL reads 2010-era; FFmpeg-plus-Vimeo reads as operating an encoder,
  not building a pipeline; OMAF is a real standard with little commercial
  uptake.
- Current but under-sold: Gaussian splatting, the mesh-to-SFU move (PeerJS to
  Ant Media Server), and the agent-driven port.

WHAT A HIRING MANAGER WOULD PROBE

1. Team size and your exact scope versus the platform's.
2. Quest 2 frame budget and memory; how episode load and unload worked.
3. Why your own WebGL library, and why Three.js after it; how you render
   splats; what you would do with WebGPU.
4. The Colyseus state model, reconnection, why Ant Media, and what breaks at
   200 participants rather than 20.
5. Syncing 360° video with 3D; adaptive bitrate and HLS/DASH; what you actually
   did on OMAF.
6. How the TypeScript port was verified with no test suite, and how you review
   agent output.
7. Production React and CI.
8. When the iOS/tvOS clients last shipped.
9. Why no Staff title after a decade.

STRONG FIT

- Segment 1, web side: WebGL, Three.js and WebXR roles, experiential studios,
  3D viewers, Gaussian-splat viewers, web 3D mapping.
- Segment 2, player side: web and 360° video players, WebRTC client work.
- Segment 3's deep end: canvas surfaces, multiplayer editors, node-based
  editors — strong on the work, weak on the React and testing screens.

STRETCH

- C++ engine, rendering or GPU-driver roles.
- Codec and pipeline roles.
- Segment 4 without recent, named AVFoundation work.
- Generic senior frontend.
- Staff at large companies.
- Medical device visualization: the anatomy work is 2009–2015, and device
  makers want regulated process and usually C++.

DOES THE FRAMING HOLD UP?

Partly. The range is real; counting it as five products is the wrong way to
claim it.

- A domain reader sees one product that grew. The iOS/tvOS apps are clients of
  the player, WebXR is a mode of it, and WebRTC video is a feature of the
  metaverse platform.
- Argue range through technical generations instead: custom WebGL → Three.js
  with composited video and splats; flat screen → headset; single viewer →
  shared sessions; JavaScript → TypeScript; hand-written code → Blockly
  authoring → AI-guided experiences.
- Gaussian splatting belongs — more than WebXR or WebRTC as separate entries.
  It is your most current technology and your bridge to 3D-capture companies.
  It is a renderer capability, not a product; surface it early.
- The TypeScript port is a modernization and current-practice story, not a
  product. Lead with it for AI-forward teams.
- Blockly is unfinished: list it as current work, not a product.
- Broadcasters make your contribution indirect. Outside media companies, lead
  with Magic of Flight, the awards and the traffic numbers.
- Cumming School of Medicine: as a lead item it says your headline work is a
  decade old. Put it high only for medical and edtech readers.
- Fraunhofer/OMAF as supporting evidence is the right weight.

The single change that moves the read most: a small public WebGPU or
react-three-fiber demo, plus one line on how you test.
