4. BLUNT READ ON MARKET POSITION
===============================================================================

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 WHERE THE TEN YEARS AT ONE SMALL COMPANY HELP
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- You own a whole stack. You built a commercial WebGL player, rewrote its
  renderer, extended it to XR, added multi-user real-time video, and
  shipped native clients on two Apple platforms. Very few candidates at
  any level can say they were the person for all of that.
- The rewrite story is your best asset. "I built a renderer, then decided
  to replace it with Three.js and executed that migration on a live
  commercial product" is a senior-scope engineering judgement story, and
  it is rarer than it sounds. Most people have only ever consumed the
  framework.
- Longevity removes an objection rather than creating one. Nobody will
  worry you are a flight risk.
- The external collaborations are real differentiators: a research
  institute (Fraunhofer HHI), a platform holder (Meta), and an academic
  partner (Conestoga). Those are cross-organizational engagements, not
  logos, and most senior candidates have none.
- Small-company breadth maps well onto three of the target clusters —
  agencies, broadcast vendors and small product companies all want
  someone who can hold the whole pipeline.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2 WHERE THEY HURT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- Scope, not loyalty, is the problem. At roughly twenty years in, the
  default expectation at a mid-size or large company is Staff or
  Principal: influence beyond your own team, technical strategy, mentoring
  a group, cross-team design review. A decade titled "Senior Developer" at
  a small company reads as ten years of senior-level individual output.
  You will most often be levelled Senior. Decide in advance whether that
  is acceptable, because arguing it in the moment goes badly.
- No calibration signal. Big-company interviewers implicitly ask "has this
  person been held to a bar?" You have no external code review culture, no
  design review process, no promotion committee in your record. This is
  the single most common reason strong small-company engineers get
  filtered at the resume stage.
- One-context tooling. Whatever your shop does is the only way you have
  seen it done. Expect probing on: branching and release process, CI, test
  strategy, incident handling, how you shipped without breaking
  customers, how work was prioritized.
- The resume shows no testing, no CI/CD, no cloud, no containerization, no
  observability, no analytics or experimentation. For Segment 3 in
  particular this is the biggest visible hole — a senior frontend posting
  at a product company will list testing and CI as table stakes.
- Comp anchoring. A decade at a small company usually means a below-market
  base. Go in with a researched Toronto number rather than a multiple of
  your current salary, and refuse to disclose the current figure.
- Recency of achievement. Everything you lead with is from 2021 or
  earlier. Read strictly, your resume's most recent dated accomplishment
  is five years old.

4.4 WHAT A HIRING MANAGER WILL PROBE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Have a crisp answer ready for each of these. The first two decide the
interview.

- "Why leave now, after ten years?" Needs to be forward-looking and free
  of any complaint about the company.
- "What did you own versus what the team owned?" Answer with team size,
  what you decided, and what would have happened if you had not been
  there.
- "Walk me through the WebGL to Three.js migration." They want the
  decision, not the diff: what forced it, what you gave up, how you kept
  the product shipping, and what it measurably bought you. Bring numbers.
  Frame time, bundle size, features unlocked, time-to-build for a new
  project.
- Rendering specifics: frame budget at 72 and 90 Hz on Quest, draw call
  and overdraw management, texture memory limits and compressed texture
  formats, stereo eye layouts for 360 video, why high-resolution
  stereoscopic 360 is hard on mobile GPUs.
- Video specifics: your bitrate ladder and why, what OMAF bought over
  plain equirectangular delivery, latency numbers, how you measured
  quality, what you would use today.
- Real-time specifics: how many concurrent users you actually supported,
  what broke first, what you would do differently than PeerJS now.
- "How do you test?" Prepare this honestly and specifically; a weak answer
  here is what turns a strong graphics interview into a no.
- Levelling probe: "describe influence outside your own team." Meta,
  Fraunhofer and Conestoga are the answer — told as collaborations you
  drove, with what you had to negotiate and integrate.
- Comp expectations, early and repeatedly.

4.6 DOES YOUR FRAMING HOLD UP?
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Mostly, but not in the form you are currently using it.

"Five distinct products, so it reads as range." Half true. It is genuine
range of surface — a web renderer, two native clients, an XR runtime, a
real-time multi-user system — but a skeptical reader hears "five features
of one platform, in one domain, at one small company." The problem is not
the claim, it is that the resume makes it unverifiable. Under Liquid
Cinema you have one date range and an undifferentiated bullet list. Nobody
skimming for six seconds can see five arcs there. Fix: break the Liquid
Cinema entry into sub-headings with their own date ranges — 360 WebGL
player (2015-2018), iOS/tvOS clients (2017-2019), WebXR (2019-2021),
WebRTC multi-user (2020-2022), collaborative platform (2021-2024), or
whatever the true dates are. That single structural change does more for
this framing than any wording will.

"Lead with the Meta Quest collaboration." Keep, but sharpen. As written,
"Meta collaboration" is ambiguous — partner, client, grantee, or featured
developer are all different things, and a reader assumes the least
impressive one. Say what the relationship actually was and what reach the
result had.

"Three 2021 WebXR Poly Awards." Real validation, with two limits. It is
five years old, and it is a community award that will land with XR
insiders and mean nothing to a frontend hiring manager at a product
company. Lead with it in Segment 1 and in agency applications. Demote it
to a single line for Segments 2 and 3.

"Fraunhofer HHI OMAF integration." Your strongest signal for Segment 2 and
close to useless everywhere else. Lead with it for video roles. Be ready
for the follow-up that OMAF did not win in the market, and answer with
what generalizes: viewport-dependent delivery, tiled streaming, projection
formats, the trade between bandwidth and seek latency.

What the framing is missing entirely: the present. Three pillars, all
dated 2021 or earlier, in a field that has moved through WebGPU,
WebCodecs, AV1, Gaussian splatting and visionOS since then. The first
thing a sharp interviewer will notice is that nothing you lead with is
recent. You need one current-era proof point before you start applying —
a small WebGPU or WebCodecs project, an AV1 pipeline experiment, a
Gaussian-splatting viewer, or an open-source contribution to Three.js.
One weekend artifact, publicly visible, fixes the largest hole in this
profile.

One more wording note: "metaverse" has aged badly and reads in some rooms
as a 2021 relic. Describe that project as a multi-user real-time 3D
collaboration platform instead. Same work, no baggage.
