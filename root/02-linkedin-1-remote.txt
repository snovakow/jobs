WHAT TO CAPTURE — LinkedIn job search is semantic, not keyword matching. A
search returns close matches first, then a divider along the lines of "we
found more results that may not be exact matches", then a long tail of
unrelated jobs. Capture only what appears before that divider. If a search
returns no close matches at all, record nothing for that keyword and move on;
do not fall back to the tail.

OVERSIZED CLOSE-MATCH SETS — check the close-match count before you start
capturing a keyword. A broad term can return so many close matches that the
divider stops filtering anything. If a keyword returns more than about 30
close matches, take the first page only (about 25) and say so in the coverage
list at the end, along with the full count.

NO DIVIDER AT ALL — expect this to be the normal case. It does not mean
every result is a close match, and it does not mean there is nothing here.
Do not substitute the total result count for a close-match count, and do not
throw the page away.

Work page 1 card by card instead — every card, Promoted or not. A Promoted
badge means someone paid to place the card. It says nothing about whether the
posting is on-topic, and it is not a reason to skip one. The same sponsored
listings do repeat across every search regardless of the term, and they drop
out on their own under the test below, because their titles name nothing to
do with this keyword. A Promoted card whose title does name it is a real
posting, and skipping it loses a lead I had no other way of seeing.

Read every title on the page. Capture a card whose title plainly names this
keyword's subject matter. Open a card whose title is ambiguous but plausibly
on-topic, within this run's open cap, and capture or drop it on what the
posting actually says. Record nothing for the keyword only when no card on
page 1 relates to it — never because the only cards that related were
Promoted.

The open cap is a budget to spend, not to protect: while you are under it,
an ambiguous card gets opened, not judged from its title. Ending a
no-divider run with the cap untouched while cards you called plausible sit
unopened is a worse failure than a wasted open.

A card captured this way still gets opened like any other — on this pass a
plain title only makes a card worth an open, never a substitute for one, and
each open counts against the twenty.

Say in the coverage list that no divider appeared, give the total count, and
name any ambiguous card you decided not to open, with its company, title and
URL. "Page 1 was all Promoted" is not a coverage outcome and never explains
an empty keyword — those cards were yours to judge like any other. The
oversized-set rule above applies only when a divider exists and sits beyond
about 30 results.
