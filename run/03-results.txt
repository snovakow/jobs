CLOSING NOTES, FIRST RUN (Segments 1 to 4)

- Patterns: Graphics/XR openings in Canada now sit mostly inside simulation and neural-rendering teams, where Gaussian splatting is the shared ground and C++, Python or PyTorch is the gate; frontend-with-depth roles ask for rendering performance on canvas or 3D viewers but pair it with Node, cloud or SDK ownership; video and Apple-platform roles ask for current Swift, SwiftUI and AVFoundation player tuning, not legacy Objective-C.
- Adjacent shapes worth not dismissing: web viewer and SDK roles at robotics-data and autonomous-driving companies, where the "video player" is a multi-stream sensor player; asset-pipeline and content-tooling roles inside simulation teams, which reward 3D asset pipelines more than ML research; and generalist senior developer roles at mapping companies whose stack names deck.gl.

CLOSING NOTES, SECOND RUN (Segments 2 and 4)

Patterns. Segment 2: the player-side roles that exist in Canada right now sit mostly with staff-augmentation networks (Kake) and public broadcasters, and they ask for named commercial player SDKs (Bitmovin, JW Player, Shaka, HLS.js) and CTV targets rather than general web skills; pipeline and transport roles (Xsolla, Solink, Bell Media's video pipeline reqs) are gated on Rust, C++, Go or AWS operations. Segment 4: Apple-platform roles on video products ask for SwiftUI, Combine and Swift Concurrency first and AVPlayer/AVFoundation second; Ring's Toronto mobile reqs are platform and app-foundation roles on a camera-video product rather than player roles.

Adjacent shapes. Video-frontend roles inside non-media companies (telematics, video security) are titled plain "Software Developer" or "Full-Stack Engineer" and are easy to skip, yet their core is a browser video experience. Mobile platform or SDK roles at camera-video companies are another shape worth reading past the title, since streaming protocols appear as preferred qualifications. Bilingual public-broadcaster OTT roles carry the closest product match but hinge on French and contract terms.
